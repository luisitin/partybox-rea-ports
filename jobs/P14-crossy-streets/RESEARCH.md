# P14 Crossy Streets research

Research date: 2026-10-11. This study uses official developer material, independent hands-on reports of the original, and read-only inspection of public implementations. No source code, image, model, sound, font, logo, or character from those sources is copied into the port.

## Evidence and limits

The official [Crossy Road site](https://www.crossyroad.com/) describes an endless hopper across roads, railways and rivers. The original developers' [GDC 2015 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2015/presentations/Hall_Matthew_Crossy_Road_Whale.pdf), pages 18–20 and 25, identify procedural generation, difficulty, session length, a mix of tapping and swiping, and amusing deaths as deliberate design work. Neither publishes the exact terrain probabilities or the complete generation algorithm.

[Carter Dotson's TouchArcade review](https://toucharcade.com/2014/11/21/crossy-road-review/) and [Rob Rich's Pocket Gamer review](https://www.pocketgamer.com/crossy-road/review/) are two independent launch-period hands-on accounts. They corroborate random terrain, single-space cardinal hops, distance chasing, and the eagle punishing a character that falls behind. The research worker did not directly play the original. These are attributed observations, not measurements made during this job.

Exact original hop duration, traffic speeds, lane weights, eagle timeout and collision tolerance were not established. Published fan descriptions disagree about idle seconds and whether sideways movement resets the eagle. The port must advertise its own threshold and test it rather than present an uncertain number as an original rule.

## Public client inspection: generation and movement

Read [lowthergv/crossy](https://github.com/lowthergv/crossy), specifically [world.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lowthergv/crossy/main/src/world.js), [lanes.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lowthergv/crossy/main/src/lanes.js), [player.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lowthergv/crossy/main/src/player.js), [game.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lowthergv/crossy/main/src/game.js) and [constants.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lowthergv/crossy/main/src/constants.js). This is an independent remake, not leaked original code. Its implementation demonstrates:

- A safe opening and occasional safe grass rows make random terrain readable; bounded hazard runs avoid an uninterrupted wall of dangers.
- A road lane owns a direction and speed; spaced vehicles wrap beyond the field, so movement remains continuous.
- Logs are moving support: landing without support drowns, and standing on a log carries the player sideways toward an edge.
- Progress stores the furthest reached row. Retreating and returning do not add score. New maximum progress resets idle pressure.
- The moving body and hazard agree about position, including hop transitions, so death corresponds to contact.

Those are observations of that remake only. They suggest invariants to test, not constants to reuse. Independent confirmation of the broader row/tile model and freely moving traffic comes from [Hunor Márton Borbély's authored Three.js tutorial](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/how-to-code-a-crossy-road-game-clone-with-threejs). The [Gamefroot tutorial](https://gamefroot-developer.gitbooks.io/crossy-road-tutorial/content/step-3.html) independently organizes generation into road, river and tree sections. Both are educational implementations, not evidence of the proprietary generator.

## Adaptation for eight people

The TV is one wide public map with player numbers, names and distinct shapes. Phones provide four directions plus the player's number, furthest row and explicit elimination cause. Players can occupy the same space: removing body collisions keeps all eight exposed to the same course and prevents a teammate from blocking the route. Map movement and terrain are seeded once for everyone. The camera must still let a trailing player identify their location; leader-only camera movement must not create an unadvertised death rule.

The course should preserve safe openings, bounded groups of roads/rivers and crossable waiting opportunities. Difficulty comes from route choice and timing, with no unavoidable spawn collision. All lanes, hazard positions and death judgments derive from state plus event time; a late timer must not skip collisions. The game contract's pause and reconnect behavior must preserve the same world and scores.

Phone-only rooms need the actual public map above the direction controls; spectators need the map without buttons. Score and warnings use text and shapes as well as colour. Idle danger should show a word and a shrinking allowance before the bird arrives. Art is original geometric road, river, vehicle, log, bird and racer drawing, using PartyBox tokens. The port does not recreate the original collectible economy. Any sound comes from available SDK cues, with no original recording or claim of measured original sound timing.

The implementation's chosen width, hop cadence, idle warning/catch thresholds, hazard speed and spacing, scoring ties, pause behavior and final phase durations belong in the game README and tests. Research recommendations do not substitute for verification or direct-play evidence.
